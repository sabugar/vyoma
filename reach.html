India has 142.34 crore people and this device does not serve all of them; we have never claimed it does. What it serves is one worker, at one doorstep, answering one question — and the people on the other side of that question are mothers and their children. This is who they are, how many, and where they live.

One number decides where a device like this belongs: 73.2% of all infant deaths in India are neonatal — they happen in the first 28 days, and most of those in the first 48 hours. That is precisely the window in which no doctor is present, the ASHA is in the home, the decision is hers, and her only reference is a printed book she cannot search while holding a baby.

An urban Indian has about two and a half connections for every one a rural Indian has. And that is the average, not the edge: the Government’s own 4G Saturation project was created for 24,680 villages with no coverage at all, and of 39,799 rural and remote uncovered villages planned for 4G through Digital Bharat Nidhi, 33,044 had been covered by 2025 — leaving about 6,755 villages still waiting. An ASHA posted in one of them cannot use a cloud assistant on any day, and an ASHA posted anywhere rural cannot rely on one on the day it matters.

This device answers with the network switched off. Speech recognition, translation, retrieval, the language model and the voice all run on the board in the ASHA’s hand. There is no data charge, no signal to find, and nothing about the mother or child leaves the device.

At 2.61 crore births across 10.29 lakh ASHAs, one ASHA attends about 25 births a year. Over a five-year service life that is roughly 127 mothers and 127 newborns from a single device.

The last line is the one that compounds. There is no per-query cost, no subscription and no bandwidth, so asking the device a hundred questions in a day costs exactly what asking it none costs. A cloud assistant is charged for every answer it gives, forever.

Where it goes nextthree tiers, honestly separated

Same device, more of the book no new hardware, no new software

Today the device carries one of the seven ASHA modules — Module 7, child health and nutrition. The remaining six are published by the same ministry in the same form. Adding them is a content task: it widens what she can ask about to antenatal care, delivery, postnatal care, nutrition, communicable and non-communicable disease, without touching the hardware or the pipeline. This is the single largest improvement available and it is the next thing we will do.

Same device, another language a model swap

Each unit carries one language pack. BHASHINI already publishes recognition, translation and speech models for every scheduled language, so a device for Marathi, Bengali, Tamil or Odia is the same board with different weights on it — not a new build, not a new app, and not a new procurement. A district that speaks two languages buys two packs, not two products.

Same device, another worker ANM, Anganwadi worker, MPW

The ASHA is not the only frontline worker carrying a manual she cannot search. The Auxiliary Nurse Midwife, the Anganwadi worker and the multi-purpose worker each have their own printed guidance and the same problem with it. The device does not know it is talking to an ASHA; it knows what book it is holding.

Needs new work, and in one case approval not claimed as ready

Camera-based screening. The board has a camera the current application does not use. The clearest candidate is newborn jaundice from skin colour, which an ASHA currently judges by eye in poor light. This is a medical screening claim and would need Ministry of Health approval and a proper clinical validation before it goes near a mother — we are not claiming it works today.

Beyond health. Nothing in the architecture is medical. It is an offline machine that answers questions in an Indian language from a book it has been given. The same device with a different book serves agricultural extension, entitlement and scheme eligibility, or school teaching guidance — wherever a worker in a low-connectivity district has to act on a manual she cannot search.

What we do not claim

This does not serve 142 crore Indians. Today’s scope is mothers and children, and the knowledge base is one module of seven. A question about antenatal care has no answer in the book the device carries, and it is built to say so rather than guess.

The reach figures above are the population the ASHA network is positioned to serve, not users acquired. They are what one device is worth if it is in her hand, multiplied by how many hands there are. Nothing here counts a deployment that has not happened.

The mortality figures are not a promise of lives saved. They say where the deaths are and when they happen, which is why the ASHA’s doorstep is the right place for this. Proving an effect on those numbers needs a field study, and that comes after a pilot, not before it.
